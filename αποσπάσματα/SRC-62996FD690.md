# Evidence — Uncertainty-Based Out-of-Distribution Detection in Deep Reinforcement Learning

## E1 — Epistemic uncertainty can be used as an OOD signal in value-based deep RL
- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Abstract; Section 1
- **Claim:** The paper applies approximate Bayesian and ensemble uncertainty estimation directly to the value-estimating network and uses epistemic uncertainty to distinguish in-distribution from OOD states.
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase
Sedlmeier et al. motivate their detector through the connection between epistemic uncertainty and sparse training-data coverage. Because epistemic uncertainty reflects lack of knowledge rather than irreducible environment randomness, states far from the training-data support are expected to produce larger uncertainty estimates and can therefore be treated as OOD candidates.

### Context and limits
The relationship is an uncertainty heuristic implemented with deep neural approximations. The resulting score is not automatically a calibrated probability of environmental change.

### Thesis use
Use uncertainty as an optional detector input only after independent threshold calibration and sequential false-alarm/delay evaluation.

### Citation
Sedlmeier et al. (2019), Abstract and Section 1.

## E2 — The GridWorld experiment separates training and OOD evaluation data
- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Section 3, Experimental Setup
- **Claim:** The agent is trained only in one GridWorld configuration and evaluated independently in a mirror configuration that generates states absent from training; evaluation data are not fed back into learning.
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase
Training episodes start in one half of a `12 x 4` grid and terminate at goals in the other half. For OOD evaluation, start and goal regions are reversed, producing a mirror environment intended to visit states outside the training distribution. The evaluation runs use model snapshots and their trajectories are not added to the training data.

### Context and limits
This is a static held-out train/test shift, not an online changepoint inside a continuing trajectory.

### Thesis use
Use held-out structural configurations for leakage-safe OOD diagnostics, but evaluate online recovery separately.

### Citation
Sedlmeier et al. (2019), Section 3.

## E3 — Bootstrap uncertainty was more stable than the tested dropout variants in this experiment
- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Sections 4–5; Figure 1
- **Claim:** Bootstrap and bootstrap-prior networks maintained a more reliable train-versus-mirror uncertainty separation over training than the tested MC Dropout and Concrete Dropout variants.
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase
Across the reported training process, the bootstrap-based estimators tend to produce declining uncertainty on the familiar training environment while retaining higher uncertainty on the mirror environment. The two dropout-based approaches show reversals or inconsistent separation, making a fixed threshold less reliable in this setting. Adding a randomized prior increases the separation for the bootstrap model in the reported experiment.

### Context and limits
The authors explicitly note that further environments are needed to determine whether the result generalizes. It is not evidence of universal ensemble superiority.

### Thesis use
Select an uncertainty estimator through held-out calibration and sequential metrics rather than theoretical popularity alone.

### Citation
Sedlmeier et al. (2019), Sections 4–5 and Figure 1.

## E4 — Aleatoric and epistemic uncertainty must remain separate
- **Type:** faithful paraphrase
- **Location:** Section 1; Discussion
- **Claim:** Aleatoric uncertainty represents irreducible stochasticity, whereas epistemic uncertainty arises from limited knowledge and can decrease as data accumulate.
- **Status:** verified

### Faithful paraphrase
The detector rationale depends specifically on epistemic uncertainty increasing in low-data-density regions. The authors note that extensions to stochastic environments require separating this reducible uncertainty from aleatoric variability so ordinary stochastic outcomes are not automatically interpreted as novelty.

### Thesis use
Do not threshold total prediction variance as a change signal without checking whether the score is dominated by irreducible environment noise.

### Citation
Sedlmeier et al. (2019), Section 1 and Discussion.

## E5 — OOD uncertainty is not a sequential changepoint decision rule
- **Type:** scope inference grounded in the experiment
- **Location:** Sections 3–5
- **Claim:** The paper evaluates separation between train and mirror-environment uncertainty distributions but does not score an online detector for alarm timing or false alarms over a continuous stream.
- **Status:** verified

### Thesis use
A sequential detector built from epistemic uncertainty still requires a declared aggregation window, threshold, cooldown/reset semantics, false-alarm rate, detection delay, and missed-change rate.

### Citation
Sedlmeier et al. (2019), experimental setup and results.

## E6 — Detection does not specify adaptation
- **Type:** thesis-scope synthesis
- **Location:** Overall paper
- **Claim:** Identifying an OOD state can support intervention or escalation, but the paper does not define how an RL policy should relearn after an environmental shift.
- **Status:** verified

### Thesis use
Keep uncertainty estimation, change declaration, and adaptation response as separate modules and ablations.

### Citation
Sedlmeier et al. (2019), overall scope.
